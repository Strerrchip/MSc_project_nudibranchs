Automated image-analysis workflow plan

Project aim

My project uses D03 toxicity, unpalatability and chemical defence data as the main biological dataset. I want to develop an automated image-analysis workflow to extract colour, pattern and animal-background contrast traits from citizen-science images of dorid nudibranchs.

Reason for changing the workflow

The Fiji workflow was useful as a pilot, because it helped me understand what measurements I could extract from the images. However, it required manual ROI drawing and manual measurements. Since this is a Computational Methods project, the final workflow should be more automated and reproducible.

Role of the manual Fiji data

The manual Fiji measurements will not be the final method. I will keep them as a small validation or comparison dataset. They can be used to check whether the automated measurements give similar results.

Planned automated workflow

1. Read images from the species folders.
2. Automatically separate the nudibranch from the background.
3. Create an animal mask.
4. Create a surrounding background ring by expanding the animal mask and subtracting the animal area.
5. Extract animal-only colour and pattern metrics.
6. Extract background metrics from the surrounding background ring.
7. Calculate animal-background contrast metrics.
8. Save image-level results to a CSV file.
9. Summarise image-level results at species level.
10. Compare species-level image traits with D03 chemical defence variables.

Background definition

The background will be defined as a ring around the animal, rather than a separate manually selected area.

The idea is:

background ring = expanded animal mask - animal mask

This should make the background choice more consistent, because the background pixels are taken from close to the animal boundary.

Possible segmentation methods to test

I will first test methods that can automatically segment the animal from the image. Possible options include:

* OpenCV or scikit-image methods
* colour clustering
* edge-based segmentation
* Segment Anything / SAM or SAM2
* Cellpose or ilastik if useful

The most promising option may be to use a general segmentation model such as SAM/SAM2 to produce candidate masks, and then use Python rules to select the most likely nudibranch mask.

Possible animal-only metrics

These describe the animal itself. They are related to the idea of boldness from D05.

Examples:

* animal area
* animal RGB mean values
* animal brightness mean
* animal brightness variation
* animal saturation mean
* animal saturation variation
* colour variation within the animal
* possible edge or texture metrics

Possible animal-background metrics

These describe how different the animal is from the nearby background. They are related to the idea of detectability from D05.

Examples:

* RGB contrast
* brightness contrast
* saturation contrast
* colour distance between animal and background
* possible boundary contrast

Outputs

For each image, I want to save:

* animal mask
* background ring
* QC figure showing the original image, animal mask and background ring
* image-level metric row in a CSV file

Suggested output folders:

processed_data/automated_image_analysis/masks/
processed_data/automated_image_analysis/background_rings/
processed_data/automated_image_analysis/qc_figures/
processed_data/automated_image_analysis/image_metrics/
processed_data/automated_image_analysis/validation/

Validation

I will compare the automated outputs with the existing manual Fiji measurements for a small number of images.

Possible comparisons:

* manual brightness contrast vs automated brightness contrast
* manual saturation contrast vs automated saturation contrast
* manual RGB contrast vs automated RGB contrast
* qualitative comparison between manual ROI and automated mask

This will help me check whether the automated workflow is reasonable.

Connection with D03

After extracting image traits, I will summarise them at species level and merge them with D03 variables, such as:

* chemical defence class
* toxicity / LD50
* unpalatability / ED50
* compound type

Then I can explore whether animal-only traits and animal-background traits are related to toxicity, unpalatability or chemical defence class.

Important limitation

Citizen-science images are not colour-calibrated. Therefore, I should be careful when interpreting absolute colour values. The main focus should be on relative image-derived traits, especially within-image animal-background contrast and animal-only pattern variation.

Next immediate step

Test automatic segmentation on a small set of images that already have manual Fiji measurements.
